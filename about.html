The story

From renunciation to relationship with the world.

Not a resume, but the living story behind the guidance: what led to monastic life, what practice revealed, and why this work is now offered to others.

The movement toward monastic life began with a longing for truth that would not quiet down. Ordinary answers could not reach the place where the question lived. That longing became a call toward renunciation, discipline, scripture, meditation, and service.

In the early years, practice brought structure. It asked for humility, patience, and the willingness to see how much of suffering comes from mistaken identity. Over time, silence became less of an escape and more of a doorway into steadiness.

Through 27 years of Advaita Vedanta practice, the teaching became increasingly direct: the peace we seek is not an achievement. It is obscured by misunderstanding and revealed through inquiry.

Now the intention is to help sincere seekers meet their own questions with honesty, tenderness, and precision. Spiritual life does not need to become another performance. It can become a clear way of living.

The work is not to improve the self endlessly. The work is to know what the self truly is.

Guidance begins with listening.

If your own questions feel ready for sincere attention, begin with a complimentary conversation.
